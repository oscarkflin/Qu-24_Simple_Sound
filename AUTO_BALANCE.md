# Auto balance pilot — Android 0.8.0

This is **experimental calibrated level hold**, not an autonomous sound engineer. It maintains each participating source near a mix you approved. It does not listen to the room, equalize instruments to the same level, recognize a voice, tune EQ, fix gain, or protect against acoustic feedback. Supervise the first church test before a service; the real church Qu-24 meter stream has not yet been validated.

## Turn it on

1. In Demo, save your actual instrument/channel patch in Options. Example assignments cannot enable Auto through the UI.
2. Find/connect to the Qu-24, check complete state synchronization, and enable live channel control.
3. Select Speaking, Worship, or Together. This is a normal service-mode action and changes channel mutes as previously documented.
4. Set a good starting mix by listening. Tap **Check meters · no fader changes** on the home screen. The check subscribes to meters but does not adjust faders.
5. Speak/play the channels you want maintained steadily for at least 10 seconds. Check meter readings on the physical desk during the first hardware test. Unknown, malformed, stale, or absent data keeps Auto off.
6. When ready, tick **This mix sounds right. Use it as the reference.**, then tap **Auto balance — OFF** to turn it on. The switch shows ON and reports the number of participating channels. Turning on itself makes no fader changes.

Only sources eligible for the selected service mode participate. They must be assigned, unmuted, have known input faders between approximately −40 and −3 dB, and show meter activity at or above −45 dB for at least 70% of calibration samples (at least 10 valid readings). An entire multi-channel patch source is excluded if any of its channels fails these checks. Quiet/inactive sources are excluded; there is no later automatic enrollment or unmuting.

## What it changes

The reference combines each input's post-delay meter and its LR input-fader level. During operation it uses approximately three seconds of recent readings and a 3 dB deadband. It changes at most one patch source every five seconds, one MIDI fader-code step per channel. A code step is non-linear and can approach 1.25 dB near the lower calibration limit; it is not exactly 1 dB. Channels belonging to one patch source move together. Every target is limited to ±3 dB of its approved starting fader, and never above 0 dB. Silence, source dropouts, and insufficient recent samples are skipped rather than boosted.

Only LR input faders can be changed. No gain, EQ, master, monitor-send, routing, mute, or FX-return changes are generated by Auto. Any post-fader-dependent routing may nevertheless follow input-fader movement: verify your monitor sends and routing at the desk.

## Turn it off / take over

- Tap the ON switch to stop. Current faders are kept. A command already sent cannot be unsent; its readback may still arrive.
- **Undo last adjustment**, after Auto has stopped, returns all confirmed automatic fader changes from that Auto session to their starting positions. It refuses if those faders have since changed externally. Starting a new Auto session adopts a new reference and discards that earlier Auto Undo.
- Normal app actions stop Auto before proceeding, including service-mode changes, level requests, Undo, and Mute all. Manual input-fader or input-mute changes reported by the desk also stop it. Controls other than the Auto off switch can be briefly busy while waiting for a command readback.
- No valid meters for more than three seconds, delayed native delivery, malformed MIDI/meters, a preamp or LR output meter at/above −3 dB, a missing command readback, disconnect, or leaving the foreground stops Auto. No automatic restart occurs. Reconnect/check/approve again as appropriate.

External gain/EQ, DCA, master, routing and other desk controls are **not tracked as manual-takeover events** in this pilot. Stop Auto before engineering changes or recalling scenes. Meter-based level hold cannot reliably distinguish a musician getting quieter from an intentional performance change, leakage, or a gain/processing change. Keep someone listening and keep the physical console available.

## Protocol and diagnosis

Implemented from [A&H Qu Classic MIDI protocol V1.9+ ISS.2](https://www.allen-heath.com/content/uploads/2023/06/Qu_MIDI_Protocol_V1.9.pdf), pages 10–12: meter subscription SysEx command `12`, reply `13`, 7-bit unpacking, big-endian 7Q8 offset `8000`. The decoder accepts the Qu-24's exact 830-meter layout (1,660 unpacked bytes); other layouts fail closed. Control commands still use negotiated audio MIDI channel and per-command readback.

Share the usual diagnostic report if anything looks wrong. It includes Auto state, eligible channel IDs, periodic decoded input/peak summaries, enable/stop reasons, requested corrections, and readback confirmations. No audio is recorded or uploaded. Raw RX traces are sampled to at most one chunk per five seconds while meters are subscribed so high-rate meters do not immediately overwrite all history; raw exports are therefore not a complete packet capture. Standard command/confirmation logs remain available. Review before sharing.

Verification so far: deterministic meter/protocol/automation tests, Android build, and a USB simulated-mixer test on the S23. This is not proof of meter scaling or audible results on the actual church desk. Check the real meter readings and behavior under supervision before considering Auto during a service.
